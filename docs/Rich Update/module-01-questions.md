# Module 1, Beer Pouring: new questions for review

Nine questions in the new formats, written only from the module's existing eight True/False statements, so no new facts are added. They are in the code (`lib/questions/module-01.ts`) but switched off (`reviewed: false`) until you approve them. Once approved, a Beer Pouring run mixes them with the True/False statements.

For each one: approve, change the wording, or drop it. The correct answer is marked **(right)**.

## 1. Choose one (warm glass)
Why does a warm glass straight from the glasswasher ruin a beer?
- **The heat drives the CO2 out, so the beer pours flat and foams (right)**
- The heat makes the beer taste sweeter
- Leftover detergent reacts with the hops
- It doesn't, as long as the glass is dry

## 2. Spot the mistake (tap nozzle)
A new starter talks you through their pour. Which step breaks the health code?
- I take a cold, clean glass from the fridge
- I hold it at 45 degrees under the tap
- **I keep the nozzle in the beer so it doesn't splash (right)**
- I straighten the glass as it fills

## 3. Choose one (schooner size)
In most Australian states, how much does a schooner hold?
- 285 ml
- **425 ml (right)**
- 570 ml
- 375 ml

## 4. A number (schooner size)
A guest in Sydney orders a schooner. How many millilitres should they get? **425 ml**, exact.

## 5. Spot the mistake (slow tap)
Which part of this pour causes the most foam?
- Starting with a cold glass
- **Opening the tap slowly, a little at a time (right)**
- Tilting the glass to 45 degrees
- Closing the tap once the head reaches the line

## 6. A number (head)
How many centimetres of head should a schooner have, at most? **1.5 cm**, exact.

## 7. Pick the best line (buttery beer)
A regular says their lager tastes a bit like butter. What's the best thing to say?
- "That's just how this one tastes, mate."
- **"Thanks for telling me. Let me swap that for you, and I'll get the line checked." (right)**
- "Nobody else has said anything about it."
- "Want to try something else instead?"

## 8. Put in order (pouring a schooner)
1. Check the glass is cold and clean
2. Hold the glass at 45 degrees under the tap
3. Open the tap fully in one movement
4. Straighten the glass as it fills
5. Close the tap with a 1 to 1.5 cm head

## 9. Choose one (overflow)
Why shouldn't beer run down the outside of the glass?
- **It wastes product, hurts the venue's GP and leaves the guest a sticky glass (right)**
- It's fine, as long as you wipe the glass after
- It warms the beer up
- It only matters for craft beer

## Points to check

- **Number 4:** says "Sydney" so the answer is clear. South Australia's schooner is a different size.
- **Number 6:** only an answer of exactly 1.5 counts. Should 1 also count? The statement says 1 to 1.5 cm.
- **Number 8:** this is the pour order I took from the statements. Change it if your venues teach it differently.
