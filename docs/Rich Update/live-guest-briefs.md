# Live Guest: the guests that are live

18 guests. Each one is a short typed conversation of 3 to 6 replies. The staff member sees the scene and the guest's first line. The AI plays the guest from the rest of the brief, which the staff member only sees in the reveal afterwards, along with what the guest was thinking at each turn.

A conversation passes at 75 out of 100. Breaking the rule that can't be broken ends it straight away and is never a pass.

**The four dials** show in the reveal: the guest's mood, and speed, safety and money when the situation puts them at stake. Money counts anything given away or made again at cost, and an extra sale at menu price. The figures are rough, and each thing counts once.

**How patient the guest is** follows the person's progress. A beginner gets a patient guest (starts one mood better, forgives a slip). Once the module is mastered the guest plays as written. Once the module is mastered and the guest passed before, or 10 modules are mastered, the guest is tough (starts one mood worse, pushes back once, brings the twist in early).

## The flat schooner (Dave, module 1)

*gentle, starts annoyed. From: AI Arena, module 1. Id: `flat-schooner`.*

**Staff see before they start:**

- The scene: Friday, 6:30pm. The front bar is busy but moving. Dave, a regular, comes back to the bar holding a schooner that's mostly head.
- Dave opens with: "Mate, this one's all froth and it tastes flat. Can you sort it out?"

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Dave, a tradie in his 50s and a regular for years. Friendly by nature, short sentences, Aussie slang. He doesn't like a fuss and just wants a decent beer.
- **Wants:** A properly poured fresh schooner, quickly, without having to argue or pay again.
- **Calms them:**
  - A simple apology and a fresh one poured straight away
  - Mentioning a cold glass or pouring it properly
  - Being quick and friendly, a bit of easy banter
  - Using his name once he's given it
- **Sets them off:**
  - Saying the beer is fine, or that's just how it pours
  - Making him wait, or asking him to pay again
  - Blaming the keg, the glass washer or another staff member
  - A long technical explanation
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** Once a fresh beer is offered, he mentions his mate's beer from the same tap was the same a minute ago and asks if the keg's on its way out.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** none
- **A good ending:** Dave has a good fresh schooner, his mate's beer is sorted too, and he knows someone will check that tap or tell the manager.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: in play, starts at "Steady".
- Safety: not in play.
- Money: in play. What can happen:
  - A fresh schooner, on the house: about -$3
  - His mate's beer replaced too: about -$3

## One more round (Jess, module 15)

*hard, starts happy. From: Scenario Training, bartending 9. Id: `birthday-shots`.*

**Staff see before they start:**

- The scene: Saturday, 11:40pm, a busy bar. Jess has been here with friends for hours. Her speech is slurred and she bumped a bar stool getting up. She waves you over.
- Jess opens with: "Heyyy, can we get six tequilas? It's my birthday! Come on, just one more round."

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Jess, 26, celebrating her birthday with friends. Cheerful and persuasive. She's embarrassed and then cranky if she's refused in front of her friends, but softens if she's treated with respect.
- **Wants:** Shots for the group, and not to be embarrassed in front of her friends.
- **Calms them:**
  - Being refused kindly and quietly, without a lecture
  - Happy birthday wishes, warmth
  - Pointing to the law or the venue's licence, not a personal judgement of her
  - Offering water, food, a soft drink or a mocktail
  - Helping her friends get her home safely, such as a taxi or a ride
- **Sets them off:**
  - Being called drunk loudly or in front of her friends
  - Lecturing or sarcasm
  - Giving in partly, such as "just one then"
  - Vague answers that sound like maybe
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** After she's refused, she says her friend will order the round instead and she'll only have one.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** Jess is unduly intoxicated. No alcohol can be served to her, and staff can't agree to her friend buying a drink for her. Water, food and soft drinks are fine.
- **A good ending:** Jess accepts water or a soft drink, feels respected, and nobody serves her more alcohol.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: not in play.
- Safety: in play, starts at "At risk".
- Money: in play. What can happen:
  - Soft drinks or mocktails for the group instead: about +$30
- Money note shown in the reveal: Serving an intoxicated guest can bring fines in the thousands of dollars, for the staff member and the venue, and puts the venue's licence at risk.

## A glass for a scoop (Mia, module 24)

*gentle, starts neutral. From: AI Arena, module 24. Id: `glass-for-a-scoop`.*

**Staff see before they start:**

- The scene: Saturday, 9pm, a busy bar. You're the senior bartender. Mia, a new casual on her second shift, is scooping ice into a jug with a schooner glass because she can't find the scoop.
- Mia opens with: "I couldn't find the scoop, so I'm using this. That's fine, right?"

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Mia, 18, on her second shift. Eager to help and worried about getting things wrong. She remembers a reason better than a rule.
- **Wants:** To keep up with the rush and not look silly.
- **Calms them:**
  - Stopping her kindly and quietly, away from the guests
  - A simple reason: glass chips disappear into ice, and ice is food
  - Showing her where the scoop lives, in its clean holder
  - Checking the glass for chips, and burning the well if it's chipped
  - Encouragement, then back to service
- **Sets them off:**
  - Telling her off in front of guests
  - "It's just the rule" with no reason
  - Taking over without explaining
  - Letting it go because it's busy
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** She asks whether she can just use her hand with a glove on instead.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** Mia can't keep scooping with the glass, or with her hand, gloved or not. If the glass is chipped, the well has to be burned.
- **A good ending:** Mia is using the scoop, knows why glass and hands never go in the ice, and the glass has been checked for chips.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: in play, starts at "Steady".
- Safety: in play, starts at "Watch it".
- Money: not in play.

## A heavy hand (Lachie, module 31)

*tricky, starts neutral. From: AI Arena, module 31. Id: `heavy-hand`.*

**Staff see before they start:**

- The scene: Thursday, 11pm, and the rush has just eased. All night you've watched Lachie, another bartender, free-pour spirits about 10 to 15ml over each time. The manager has said liquor costs are up and nobody knows why.
- Lachie opens with: "You've been watching me pour all night. Something wrong?"

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Lachie, in his 20s, a confident bartender who learnt to free-pour at his last venue and is proud of it. Friendly, but touchy about his skills.
- **Wants:** To keep pouring his way and keep the regulars happy.
- **Calms them:**
  - Raising it privately, without making it about his skill
  - Real numbers: a few ml over each drink adds up to litres a night
  - The jigger protects him too, if a guest says they were short-poured
  - That it's venue policy, and any extra needs a manager's approval
  - Offering to raise the costs with the manager together
- **Sets them off:**
  - Accusing him of stealing
  - Telling him he's a bad bartender
  - Going over his head without saying anything to him first
  - Vague hints
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** He says the regulars love his pours and tip him well for them.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** none
- **A good ending:** Lachie agrees to use the jigger for every pour, understands what the over-pours cost, and knows it isn't about his skill.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: not in play.
- Safety: not in play.
- Money: in play. What can happen:
  - Lachie uses the jigger for every pour: about +$500 a week
- Money note shown in the reveal: 12ml over on 200 drinks a night is 2.4 litres of spirit, about $100 at cost. Over five nights, that's about $500 a week.
