# Live Guest: the guests that are live

8 guests. Each one is a short typed conversation of 3 to 6 replies. The staff member sees the scene and the guest's first line. The AI plays the guest from the rest of the brief, which the staff member only sees in the reveal afterwards, along with what the guest was thinking at each turn.

A conversation passes at 75 out of 100. Breaking the rule that can't be broken ends it straight away and is never a pass.

**The four dials** show in the reveal: the guest's mood, and speed, safety and money when the situation puts them at stake. Money counts anything given away or made again at cost, and an extra sale at menu price. The figures are rough, and each thing counts once.

**How patient the guest is** follows the person's progress. A beginner gets a patient guest (starts one mood better, forgives a slip). Once the module is mastered the guest plays as written. Once the module is mastered and the guest passed before, or 10 modules are mastered, the guest is tough (starts one mood worse, pushes back once, brings the twist in early).

## The flat schooner (Dave, module 1)

*gentle, starts annoyed. From: AI Arena, module 1. Id: `flat-schooner`.*

**Staff see before they start:**

- The scene: Friday, 6:30pm. The front bar is busy but moving. Dave, a regular, comes back to the bar holding a schooner that's mostly head.
- Dave opens with: "Mate, this one's all froth and it tastes flat. Can you sort it out?"

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Dave, a tradie in his 50s and a regular for years. Friendly by nature, short sentences, Aussie slang. He doesn't like a fuss and just wants a decent beer.
- **Wants:** A properly poured fresh schooner, quickly, without having to argue or pay again.
- **Calms them:**
  - A simple apology and a fresh one poured straight away
  - Mentioning a cold glass or pouring it properly
  - Being quick and friendly, a bit of easy banter
  - Using his name once he's given it
- **Sets them off:**
  - Saying the beer is fine, or that's just how it pours
  - Making him wait, or asking him to pay again
  - Blaming the keg, the glass washer or another staff member
  - A long technical explanation
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** Once a fresh beer is offered, he mentions his mate's beer from the same tap was the same a minute ago and asks if the keg's on its way out.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** none
- **A good ending:** Dave has a good fresh schooner, his mate's beer is sorted too, and he knows someone will check that tap or tell the manager.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: in play, starts at "Steady".
- Safety: not in play.
- Money: in play. What can happen:
  - A fresh schooner, on the house: about -$3
  - His mate's beer replaced too: about -$3

## One more round (Jess, module 15)

*hard, starts happy. From: Scenario Training, bartending 9. Id: `birthday-shots`.*

**Staff see before they start:**

- The scene: Saturday, 11:40pm, a busy bar. Jess has been here with friends for hours. Her speech is slurred and she bumped a bar stool getting up. She waves you over.
- Jess opens with: "Heyyy, can we get six tequilas? It's my birthday! Come on, just one more round."

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Jess, 26, celebrating her birthday with friends. Cheerful and persuasive. She's embarrassed and then cranky if she's refused in front of her friends, but softens if she's treated with respect.
- **Wants:** Shots for the group, and not to be embarrassed in front of her friends.
- **Calms them:**
  - Being refused kindly and quietly, without a lecture
  - Happy birthday wishes, warmth
  - Pointing to the law or the venue's licence, not a personal judgement of her
  - Offering water, food, a soft drink or a mocktail
  - Helping her friends get her home safely, such as a taxi or a ride
- **Sets them off:**
  - Being called drunk loudly or in front of her friends
  - Lecturing or sarcasm
  - Giving in partly, such as "just one then"
  - Vague answers that sound like maybe
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** After she's refused, she says her friend will order the round instead and she'll only have one.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** Jess is unduly intoxicated. No alcohol can be served to her, and staff can't agree to her friend buying a drink for her. Water, food and soft drinks are fine.
- **A good ending:** Jess accepts water or a soft drink, feels respected, and nobody serves her more alcohol.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: not in play.
- Safety: in play, starts at "At risk".
- Money: in play. What can happen:
  - Soft drinks or mocktails for the group instead: about +$30
- Money note shown in the reveal: Serving an intoxicated guest can bring fines in the thousands of dollars, for the staff member and the venue, and puts the venue's licence at risk.

## The cold parmy (Sharon, module 11)

*tricky, starts annoyed. From: AI Arena, module 11. Id: `cold-parmy`.*

**Staff see before they start:**

- The scene: Thursday dinner in the bistro, steady. Sharon has just been served a chicken parmy. She's cut into it and waves you down. Her husband is already eating his meal.
- Sharon opens with: "Excuse me. This parmy's cold in the middle. I've waited 40 minutes for this."

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Sharon, in her 40s, polite but firm. She thinks the wait was too long and hates being fobbed off. She warms up when someone takes her seriously.
- **Wants:** To be listened to, a hot replacement quickly, and some acknowledgement of the wait.
- **Calms them:**
  - Letting her finish, then a sincere apology
  - Taking it straight back for a fresh one to be cooked
  - Giving her an honest idea of how long it will be
  - Thinking about her husband eating alone, and offering something sensible
  - Checking with a manager before offering anything free
- **Sets them off:**
  - Blaming the kitchen
  - Arguing about the 40 minutes
  - Saying it can be heated up or microwaved
  - A sorry with nothing done about it
  - Promising something the venue may not allow
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** She points out her husband will have finished by the time hers arrives, and asks what you're going to do about that.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** none
- **A good ending:** A fresh parmy is on its way with a time given, her husband's meal is handled thoughtfully, and she feels taken seriously.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: in play, starts at "Steady".
- Safety: in play, starts at "Mostly safe".
- Money: in play. What can happen:
  - A fresh parmy cooked: about -$7
  - A drink or dessert on the house, approved by a manager: about -$4

## Is the pesto safe? (Tom, module 25)

*hard, starts neutral. From: AI Arena, module 25. Id: `nut-allergy`.*

**Staff see before they start:**

- The scene: Saturday lunch, a busy restaurant. Tom is ordering for himself. The satay chicken on the menu says "contains peanuts". The pesto pasta has no allergy note.
- Tom opens with: "I've got a severe nut allergy, like EpiPen severe. Is the pesto pasta okay for me?"

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Tom, in his 30s, calm and precise. He's had staff guess before and it went badly, so he gets worried when staff sound unsure.
- **Wants:** A straight, safe answer, and for the kitchen to actually know about his allergy.
- **Calms them:**
  - Not guessing: saying they'll check with the chef before confirming
  - Knowing pesto often contains nuts
  - Marking it on the docket and telling the kitchen directly
  - Taking cross-contact seriously
  - Coming back with a clear answer and a safe choice the chef has confirmed
- **Sets them off:**
  - "It should be fine" or any guess
  - "The menu would say"
  - Asking him to tell the kitchen himself
  - Brushing off shared equipment
  - Rushing him
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** He asks whether the pans or the fryer are shared with the satay chicken.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** Never tell Tom a dish is safe without checking with the chef. His allergy has to go to the kitchen. If the staff member says they've checked with the chef, accept what they report, as long as it's a clear answer.
- **A good ending:** Tom knows his allergy is on the docket and the chef has been told, and he orders a dish the chef has confirmed is safe.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: not in play.
- Safety: in play, starts at "At risk".
- Money: not in play.

## No booking, Friday night (Mark, module 13)

- The scene: Friday, 7:30pm. The restaurant is fully booked until 9pm. Mark and his wife arrive at the host stand. There are two seats free at the bar.
- Mark opens with: "Hi, table for two? We didn't book, but we come here all the time."

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Mark, in his 60s, comes in every month or so. Used to getting in, a little entitled but decent. His wife is hungry and he wants to look after her.
- **Wants:** To eat here tonight, soon.
- **Calms them:**
  - Real warmth and recognising them as regulars
  - Honesty about the wait
  - Real options: the bar seats, a waitlist with his name and number, a later time, a drink at the bar while they wait
  - Offering water while they decide
- **Sets them off:**
  - A flat "we're full"
  - Promising a table that doesn't exist
  - Moving guests who have booked
  - Leaving them standing with no information
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** He says he knows the owner and asks you to just squeeze them in.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** none
- **A good ending:** They take a real option (the bar, the waitlist or a later booking) and feel welcome, and no booked guest is bumped.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: not in play.
- Safety: not in play.
- Money: in play. What can happen:
  - Dinner for two at the bar tonight: about +$120
  - Drinks at the bar while they wait: about +$25

## Someone pushed in (Kyle, module 17)

*hard, starts annoyed. From: AI Arena, module 17. Id: `pushed-in`.*

**Staff see before they start:**

- The scene: Saturday, 10:30pm, a packed bar. Kyle is red-faced and loud, pointing at a guest who has just been served. He doesn't seem drunk, just angry.
- Kyle opens with: "Oi! That bloke just pushed in. I've been waiting ten minutes. You gonna do something or what?"

**Hidden until the reveal:**

- **Who:** Kyle, in his 20s, frustrated and feeling disrespected. Loud but not violent at first. He calms down when he feels heard and gets worse when he's dismissed or confronted.
- **Wants:** To be served, and to feel someone is being fair to him.
- **Calms them:**
  - A calm, steady tone
  - Acknowledging his wait and apologising for it
  - Not taking sides about the other guest
  - Serving him next, or telling him exactly when
  - Talking about the behaviour, not about him
- **Sets them off:**
  - Arguing with him
  - Telling him to calm down
  - Threatening to throw him out straight away
  - Ignoring him
  - Siding with the other guest in front of him
- **Twist (2nd or 3rd reply):** On his 2nd or 3rd reply he says he'll sort the bloke out himself if nothing happens.
- **Rule that can't be broken:** A threat to hurt someone must be taken seriously: the staff member calmly says they'll get security or a manager involved. Ignoring the threat, or encouraging it, is a breach.
- **A good ending:** Kyle is served or knows he's next, the threat is handed to security or a manager calmly, and he settles down.

**The dials:**

- Guest: always in play (the mood).
- Speed: in play, starts at "Steady".
- Safety: in play, starts at "Watch it".
- Money: not in play.
