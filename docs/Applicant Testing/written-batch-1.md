# Applicant test written scenarios, batch 1: bar and floor

12 scenarios. Each applicant gets four from their track, never two on the same topic. They answer in their own words, with no time limit.

The AI marks each answer from 1 to 5 on four things: process (sensible steps in a sensible order), guest care, safety (including the law), and judgement (when to involve a manager, staying within their authority). That becomes a score out of 100. It is told never to mark down spelling, grammar or English as a second language, and that length earns nothing.

The manager sees the answer in full, which points it covered, a line on what was strong and what was missing, and a question to ask in the interview.

**To approve one,** say so and it gets `reviewed: true`. To change one, mark up the wording here.

## 1. Bar: rsa refusal

*Id: `bar-round-of-shots`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Friday, 10:30pm. A regular you get on well with comes to the bar to order a round of shots for his group of six. He is slurring, holding the bar to stay steady, and has trouble getting his card out.
>
> **What do you do and say, and why?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Recognises the signs that he is intoxicated and that serving him is against the law, whoever he is
2. Refuses him alcohol clearly and politely, without embarrassing him, and offers water, a soft drink or food
3. Does not serve the round to his friends to pass to him, and watches for a mate buying on his behalf
4. Tells the manager or duty manager and the rest of the bar team, so nobody else serves him
5. Thinks about his safety: getting home, a friend to look after him, staying calm if he pushes back
6. Follows the venue's procedure for recording a refusal or incident

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Serves him one more because he is a regular or seems harmless
- Serves the round to the group knowing some of it is for him
- Refuses in a way that shames him in front of the group, or argues with him

## 2. Bar: glass in ice

*Id: `bar-glass-in-the-ice`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Saturday night, the bar is three deep. Reaching for a glass, a colleague knocks a highball against the ice well and it chips. You can't tell if any pieces went in.
>
> **Walk us through what happens next.**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Stops anyone using that ice straight away and warns the team
2. Treats the whole well as contaminated: empties or melts all of it, never just fishes out the visible pieces
3. Cleans and checks the well before refilling it with fresh ice
4. Keeps service going safely in the meantime, such as another well or a bucket of fresh ice, and tells waiting guests about a short delay
5. Gets rid of the broken glass safely, and checks any drinks just made from that well

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Picks out the pieces they can see and keeps serving
- Carries on because the bar is too busy to stop

## 3. Bar: pressure priorities

*Id: `bar-alone-and-busy`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> You're alone on the bar for ten minutes while a colleague is on break. Three groups are waiting to order, a floor staff member has a docket for a table's drinks, and the bar phone starts ringing.
>
> **How do you handle the next few minutes, and in what order?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Acknowledges everyone waiting quickly, with eye contact or a word, so they know they've been seen
2. Serves in a fair order, by who arrived first, not the loudest or a friend
3. Works efficiently: groups similar drinks, makes several at once, keeps their station stocked
4. Agrees timing with the floor staff member so the table's drinks aren't forgotten
5. Doesn't leave the bar for the phone, or asks someone else to take it
6. Calls for help from the manager or a colleague if it keeps building, and keeps standards like measures and cleanliness

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Serves friends or the loudest group first
- Skips measuring or cutting corners on safety to go faster

## 4. Bar: proof of age

*Id: `bar-no-id`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> A group in their early twenties comes to the bar. One of them looks young to you and can't produce ID. He says he left his wallet in the car, that he's 26, and that his friends can vouch for him.
>
> **What do you do and say?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Asks for acceptable proof of age, following the law in their state and the venue's policy on which ID counts
2. Politely declines to serve him alcohol without it, whatever his friends say
3. Doesn't serve his friends a drink for him, and keeps an eye on the group
4. Stays friendly and calm, offers a soft drink, and invites him back with ID
5. Involves the manager or security if he pushes back, and applies the same rule to everyone

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Serves him because he seems confident or his friends vouch for him
- Lets a friend buy him a drink
- Accepts a reason or a photo of ID that the venue doesn't accept

## 5. Bar: honest knowledge

*Id: `bar-drink-you-dont-know`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> A guest orders a classic cocktail you've never made and don't know the recipe for. The bar is busy and the guest is clearly a cocktail fan.
>
> **What do you do?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Is honest rather than guessing or bluffing
2. Checks the venue's recipe or spec, or asks a colleague who knows it
3. Talks to the guest: what they like about the drink, how long it will be, or offers something close if it can't be made
4. Makes it properly once they know it: measured, to spec
5. Learns it afterwards so they can make it next time

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Makes something up and serves it as the real thing
- Brushes the guest off or makes them feel awkward for asking

## 6. Bar: drink complaint

*Id: `bar-too-weak`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> A guest brings back a mixed drink and says it's far too weak. You made it, and you measured the spirit exactly as the venue's standard says.
>
> **How do you handle it, and why?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Listens and stays friendly, without arguing or suggesting the guest is wrong
2. Finds out what they'd like instead: less mixer, a different drink, or a double they pay for
3. Keeps to standard measures rather than free pouring extra to please them, for responsible service and cost
4. Offers a remake or a swap within what they're allowed to do, and checks with a manager before giving anything free
5. Leaves the guest feeling looked after

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Argues that they poured it correctly
- Adds extra spirit for free to keep the guest happy

## 7. Floor and front of house: allergy order

*Id: `floor-nut-allergy`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> A guest tells you she has a severe nut allergy and asks whether the pasta with pesto is safe for her. You're not sure what's in the pesto.
>
> **What do you do and say, from now until her meal arrives?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Takes it seriously and doesn't guess or reassure her before checking
2. Checks with the chef or kitchen and the venue's ingredient information, including the risk of contact with nuts in the kitchen
3. Tells her honestly what they found, including when the venue can't guarantee a dish is safe, and suggests safe options
4. Puts the allergy clearly on the order and tells the kitchen in person, following the venue's allergy procedure
5. Makes sure the right plate reaches her, such as carrying it themselves or checking with the runner
6. Knows what to do if she reacts: get help straight away and follow the venue's first aid procedure

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Says it should be fine without checking
- Suggests picking the nuts off or swapping the sauce without telling the kitchen about the allergy

>

1. Goes to the table before being called, rather than avoiding it

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Promises a time they don't know, or gives food or drink away without the authority to

## 9. Floor and front of house: own mistake

*Id: `floor-wrong-steak`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> You take a steak to a table. The guest says she ordered it rare, and it's medium. When you check the docket, you see you put it through as medium.
>
> **How do you put it right?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Owns the mistake to the guest and apologises, without blaming anyone
2. Takes it back and gets a fresh one cooked as she ordered it, with the kitchen told clearly that it's a priority
3. Thinks about the rest of the table: whether they wait or start, and keeps them updated
4. Tells the manager, who may want to offer something, rather than deciding a free item themselves

- Blames the kitchen
- Asks her to keep it

## 10. Floor and front of house: section priorities

*Id: `floor-five-tables`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Saturday, 7:45pm. In your section: a table has just been seated, one wants the bill, one is waving for more drinks, mains for another are sitting at the pass, and a fifth table is waiting for dessert menus.
>
> **What do you do first, second and third, and why?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Gets the hot food from the pass to the table first, because it spoils and the kitchen needs the pass clear
2. Greets or acknowledges the new table quickly, even just to say they'll be right back
3. Moves the bill along quickly, since it frees a table and a waiting guest is unhappy
4. Handles drinks and dessert menus in passing, or asks a colleague to help
5. Uses the team: asks for help, lets the manager know they're stretched
6. Keeps scanning the whole section rather than working one table at a time

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Leaves food sitting at the pass while they do other jobs
- Ignores tables without acknowledging them

## 12. Floor and front of house: booking full

*Id: `floor-full-tonight`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> A regular rings at 4pm asking for a table for eight at 7:30 tonight. The booking system shows you're full from 6:30 to 9.
>
> **How does the call go?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Is warm and uses their name, and checks the system properly before saying no
2. Offers real alternatives: an earlier or later time, bar or lounge seating, splitting the group, a waitlist or another night
3. Checks with the manager if there's any flexibility for a regular, without promising before they know
4. Takes and reads back the details for whatever is agreed: name, number, time, numbers, any needs
5. Ends the call with them feeling looked after even if there's no table

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Just says no and ends the call
- Promises a table that isn't there
