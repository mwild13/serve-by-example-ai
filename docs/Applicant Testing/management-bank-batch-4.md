# Applicant test management questions, batch 4

31 questions for the management role quiz in applicant tests, in 4 topics. An applicant gets 8 role questions, dealt across these topics and the matching training modules, easier ones first.

Unlike the staff module questions, these bring in facts of their own, so check each fact as well as the wording. Food safety facts follow the Food Standards Code and FSANZ guidance. The right answer is marked **(right)**; options and steps are shuffled for applicants.

For each one, say approve, change (and how), or drop. "All approved" is fine too.

## Running a shift

8 questions.

### m-briefing. Choose one

What belongs in a pre-shift briefing?

- **Bookings and functions, sold out items and specials, sections, and anything to watch for (right)**
- Last month's sales figures
- Who is in trouble from last week
- Nothing. Experienced staff don't need one

Explanation: A few minutes on what tonight looks like means fewer surprises once the doors open.

### m-plan-bookings. Choose one

Saturday has 60 covers booked for 7pm and a quiet hour either side. How do you plan the floor?

- **Staff up for the 7pm peak, tell the kitchen early, and ask the team to pace the seating if possible (right)**
- Roster the same as any Saturday
- Deal with it when it happens
- Turn away walk ins all night

Explanation: Bookings tell you when the rush is. Plan staff and kitchen timing around the peak, not the average.

### m-breaks. Choose one

It's busy and a bartender is due a meal break under their award. What do you do?

- **Make sure they get it, covering their station or moving the break a little within what the award allows (right)**
- Skip it tonight and pay them later
- Tell them to eat behind the bar
- Skip it, since it's their choice to work in hospitality

Explanation: Breaks set by the award or agreement are a legal requirement, not a favour. A busy night is planned for, not used as a reason.

### m-handover. Choose one

You're handing over to the closing manager. What do you tell them?

- **Anything open: incidents, guests refused service, problems with stock or equipment, and staff issues (right)**
- Just the takings so far
- Nothing. They'll work it out
- Only things that went wrong because of staff

Explanation: The next manager needs to know what's still live, such as someone refused service who may come back.

### m-in-charge. Choose one

Who has to be in charge of a licensed venue while it's trading?

- **Someone approved for it under the venue's licence, as that state's law requires (right)**
- Whoever has worked there longest
- Anyone over 18
- No one, as long as staff hold RSA

Explanation: Every state requires a licensee or an approved manager to be responsible while the venue trades. The rules on who qualifies differ by state.

### m-delegate. Pick the best line

You need a floor staff member to restock the bar fridges before the rush. What do you say?

- **"Can you restock the bar fridges before 6? The beer and white wine are lowest." (right)**
- "Someone should do the fridges."
- "The fridges are a mess again."
- Do it yourself so it's done properly

Explanation: A clear job, a time and the detail that matters gets it done. A general comment gets nothing done.

### m-close-order. Put in order

Put these steps for closing the venue in order.

1. Last drinks called and service stopped
2. Guests leave safely
3. Staff close their sections
4. Count the tills with a second person
5. Lock up and set the alarm

Explanation: Stop service, get everyone out safely, close down, count the money with a witness, then secure the building.

### m-fatigue. Choose one

A staff member who closed at 2am is rostered to open at 7am. What's the issue?

- **Not enough rest between shifts, which is a safety risk and may break the award (right)**
- None, if they agreed to it
- Only that they might be grumpy
- None, it's normal in hospitality

Explanation: Tired staff make mistakes with knives, hot oil and refusals. Awards usually set a minimum break between shifts.

## The licence and responsible service

8 questions.

### m-who-liable. Choose one

A bartender serves a guest who is clearly intoxicated. Who can be fined?

- **The bartender and the licensee or manager in charge (right)**
- Only the guest
- Only the bartender
- No one, if the guest asked for it

Explanation: Responsible service law reaches the person who served and whoever is responsible for the venue.

### m-back-refusal. Choose one

A guest demands you overrule a bartender who refused to serve him. You agree he's intoxicated. What do you do?

- **Back the bartender, offer water or food, and tell him calmly that he won't be served (right)**
- Serve him one to calm things down
- Tell the bartender to serve him this once
- Tell him to try the other bar

Explanation: Staff only refuse confidently if they know their manager will back them. Overruling teaches them not to bother.

### m-intox-leave. Choose one

An intoxicated guest has been refused service but wants to stay at his table. What's expected of the venue?

- **Ask him to leave and help him do it safely, such as a taxi or a friend, as liquor law expects (right)**
- He can stay as long as he isn't drinking
- Leave him be until closing
- Call the police straight away

Explanation: Liquor laws expect an intoxicated person not to stay on the premises. Helping them leave safely matters as much as asking.

### m-register. Choose one

Why record refusals of service and incidents?

- **It shows the venue acts on its licence duties, and protects staff if a decision is questioned later (right)**
- Only to use against staff
- It isn't needed if nobody was hurt
- Only for the venue's insurance

Explanation: A dated record of what happened and what staff did is evidence that the venue serves responsibly.

### m-minor-group. Choose one

A group's ID checks out except one person, who has none. The others offer to buy his drinks. What do you tell staff?

- **Nobody serves alcohol for him, and they watch the group for drinks being passed to him (right)**
- It's fine, the group has ID
- Serve them, but only beer
- It's his friends' responsibility now

Explanation: Supplying a minor, directly or through friends, is an offence for the venue and the staff member.

### m-new-rsa-staff. Choose one

A new bartender starts Friday. What must you check before they serve alcohol?

- **That they hold a current RSA certificate that's valid in your state (right)**
- That they've worked in a bar before
- Nothing, they can get it within a month
- That they know the cocktail list

Explanation: Serving alcohol without the RSA your state requires breaks the law for them and the venue. Check it before the shift.

### m-incident-order. Put in order

A guest is hurt in a scuffle. Put these steps in order.

1. Make the area safe and separate the people involved
2. Give first aid, and call 000 if it's serious
3. Stop serving those involved
4. Keep the CCTV and take witness details
5. Record it in the incident register

Explanation: Safety first, then care for the injured, then stop it starting again, then keep the evidence and record it.

### m-drunk-staff. Choose one

A staff member turns up for their shift and is clearly drunk. What do you do?

- **Take them off duty, make sure they get home safely, and talk to them privately when they're sober (right)**
- Let them work a quiet section
- Have a go at them in front of the team
- Let them work and deal with it later

Explanation: An intoxicated staff member is a safety risk and can't serve alcohol responsibly. Deal with it fairly once they're sober.

## Leading the team

7 questions.

### m-feedback. Choose one

A server keeps forgetting to check back on tables. How do you raise it?

- **Privately and soon, with a real example and what good looks like (right)**
- In front of the team so everyone learns
- Wait for their yearly review
- Give them worse sections until they work it out

Explanation: Feedback works when it's private, specific and close to the moment, with a clear picture of what to do instead.

### m-document. Choose one

You've had a second conversation with a staff member about lateness. What else should you do?

- **Write down the date, what was discussed and what was agreed, following the venue's process (right)**
- Nothing. Talking is enough
- Cut their hours without telling them why
- Tell the rest of the team about it

Explanation: Notes make the process fair and clear, for the staff member and the venue, if it goes further.

### m-harassment. Choose one

A staff member tells you a regular guest keeps making sexual comments to her. What do you do?

- **Take it seriously, keep her safe from the guest, deal with the guest, and follow the venue's policy (right)**
- Tell her it comes with the job
- Move her to another section and leave it there
- Ask her to ignore it because he spends a lot

Explanation: Employers have a duty to keep staff safe from harassment, including from guests. A good customer is no exception.

### m-staff-injury. Choose one

A staff member slips on a wet floor and hurts their wrist. After first aid, what else?

- **Record it, fix what caused it, and tell the right people under the venue's work health and safety process (right)**
- Nothing, if they keep working
- Ask them not to mention it
- Only record it if they need a doctor

Explanation: Recording and fixing the cause stops it happening to the next person, and some injuries must be reported by law.

### m-coach-line. Pick the best line

A new server froze when a guest complained. What's the best thing to say afterwards?

- **"That was a tough one. Let's go through what you could say next time." (right)**
- "You should have known what to do."
- "Don't worry, I'll handle all the complaints."
- Say nothing and hope it doesn't happen again

Explanation: Acknowledge it, then give them the words for next time. That builds confidence instead of fear.

### m-induction. Spot the mistake

A supervisor describes a new starter's first shift. Which part is wrong?

- **We skip the emergency exits and procedures, they can learn those later (right)**
- They shadow an experienced staff member
- We check their RSA before they serve
- We check in with them after the shift

Explanation: Emergency exits and procedures come on day one. Nobody should work a shift without knowing how to get everyone out.

### m-roster-fair. Choose one

Several staff complain that the same people always get the best shifts. What's a good response?

- **Look at the roster honestly, explain how shifts are given out, and share them fairly where you can (right)**
- Ignore it, the roster is your call
- Give everyone fewer shifts
- Tell them to swap among themselves

Explanation: A roster that looks unfair costs you good staff. Being open about how it's done builds trust.

## Stock, cost and cash

8 questions.

### m-cost-percent. A number

A cocktail sells for $20 and its ingredients cost $5. What's its cost as a percentage of the price?

**Answer: 25 %**, exact.

Explanation: Cost divided by price: $5 ÷ $20 = 25%. Venues set targets for this to keep a margin.

### m-wastage-log. Choose one

Why keep a wastage log?

- **To see what's being lost and why, so it can be fixed, and so stocktakes add up (right)**
- To catch staff out
- It's only needed for food
- There's no reason if the waste is small

Explanation: Recorded waste explains stock differences and shows patterns, such as a dish that's always thrown out.

### m-variance. Choose one

The stocktake shows two bottles of vodka more missing than sales and wastage explain. What do you do first?

- **Check for honest causes: counting errors, missed wastage, wrong pours, and unrecorded comps (right)**
- Assume theft and tell the team
- Adjust the count so it matches
- Ignore it, two bottles is nothing

Explanation: Most differences are counting or recording errors. Rule those out before thinking about theft.

### m-par. Choose one

What is a par level?

- **How much of an item you want on hand, so you order the difference (right)**
- The most you can ever store
- The price you pay the supplier
- How fast an item sells

Explanation: Count what's there, order up to par. It stops you running out or tying money up in too much stock.

### m-comps. Choose one

You give a table a free dessert after a long wait. What should happen in the system?

- **Record it as a comp with the reason, through the point of sale (right)**
- Nothing, it's a small thing
- Ring it up and pay for it from the tips
- Write it off at the end of the week

Explanation: Recorded comps keep stock and sales accurate, and show how often service problems are costing money.

### m-deduction. Choose one

A till is $50 short after a bartender's shift. Can you take the $50 out of their pay?

- **No. Under the Fair Work Act, deductions need the employee's written agreement and must be for their benefit (right)**
- Yes, if it was their till
- Yes, if you tell them first
- Yes, if it happens more than once

Explanation: Taking a shortfall from someone's pay without lawful authority is illegal. Investigate it and fix the process instead.

### m-cash-count. Choose one

Why count the tills with a second person at close?

- **It catches mistakes and protects the person counting if the figure is ever questioned (right)**
- It's faster
- Because you don't trust the staff
- There's no real reason

Explanation: Two people agreeing on a count protects both of them and the venue.

### m-labour-cost. Choose one

Tuesday trade is usually quiet, but you've rostered the same as Friday. What's the problem?

- **Wages are high for the sales, so labour cost eats the margin. Roster to the expected trade (right)**
- None, more staff is always better
- Only that staff will be bored
- None, if the staff want the hours

Explanation: Labour is one of a venue's biggest costs. Rostering to expected trade keeps service good and costs under control.
