# Applicant test written scenarios, batch 2: kitchen and management

12 scenarios. Each applicant gets four from their track, never two on the same topic. They answer in their own words, with no time limit.

The AI marks each answer from 1 to 5 on four things: process (sensible steps in a sensible order), guest care, safety (including the law), and judgement (when to involve a manager, staying within their authority). That becomes a score out of 100. It is told never to mark down spelling, grammar or English as a second language, and that length earns nothing.

The manager sees the answer in full, which points it covered, a line on what was strong and what was missing, and a question to ask in the interview.

**To approve one,** say so and it gets `reviewed: true`. To change one, mark up the wording here.

## 1. Kitchen: allergy change

*Id: `kitchen-allergy-at-the-pass`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Friday service, the board is full. A server comes to the pass: the guest on table 12 has just told them she has a severe sesame allergy. Her main is already being plated, and it has a sesame dressing.
>
> **What happens next in the kitchen, step by step?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Stops that plate going out, and checks every dish on the table's docket for sesame, including sauces, oils, garnishes and bread
2. Makes the dish again from scratch, never by scraping off the dressing or picking off garnish
3. Prevents cross contact: clean boards, knives, pans and surfaces, fresh gloves, and ingredients that haven't touched sesame
4. Tells the head chef and everyone on the line, and checks the venue's ingredient information rather than guessing
5. Keeps the plate separate and makes sure it reaches the right guest, such as a clearly marked plate taken by the server who knows
6. Tells front of house honestly if the kitchen can't make it safely, so the guest can choose something else

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Wipes or scrapes the dressing off and sends the same plate
- Carries on without telling anyone because the kitchen is too busy

## 2. Kitchen: cold chain

*Id: `kitchen-warm-coolroom`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> You arrive for the morning prep shift and the coolroom thermometer reads 11°C. The door was left slightly open overnight, and you don't know for how long. It's full of meat, dairy, cooked rice and prepped sauces.
>
> **What do you do, and why?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Knows that cold food should be kept at 5°C or below, and that high risk food above that for an unknown time may not be safe
2. Checks the temperature of the food itself with a probe thermometer, rather than going by the room's reading or how it feels
3. Throws out high risk food that can't be shown to be safe, following the venue's food safety plan, and doesn't use it to save money
4. Moves food that is still safe to working cold storage, and closes the door and checks the unit
5. Tells the head chef or manager straight away, and records what happened and what was thrown out
6. Makes sure the coolroom is fixed or serviced and checked again before it's used

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Uses the food because it still feels cold or smells fine
- Says nothing and carries on with prep

## 3. Kitchen: returned dish

*Id: `kitchen-sent-back`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Mid service, a server brings a steak back to the pass. The guest says it's overcooked. You cooked it, and you think it's exactly as ordered.
>
> **How do you handle it?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Stays calm and doesn't argue with the server or send a message back to the guest
2. Cooks a new one quickly as a priority, without holding up the rest of the board
3. Works with front of house on timing, so the rest of the table isn't left waiting or eating alone
4. Checks whether something needs fixing, such as the grill temperature or how doneness is being checked
5. Tells the head chef, and records the waste if the venue does

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Refuses to cook it again or argues that the guest is wrong
- Sends the same steak back out after a moment in the pan

## 4. Kitchen: short staffed

*Id: `kitchen-down-a-cook`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Saturday, 4pm. A cook calls in sick an hour before service. There are 140 covers booked, and you're now one person short on the line.
>
> **How do you get the kitchen through service?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Tells the head chef or manager straight away, and asks about calling in another cook in the venue's usual way
2. Works out what prep matters most for service and does that first
3. Shares out the sections sensibly among who is there

## 5. Kitchen: cross contamination

*Id: `kitchen-same-board`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> You notice a new junior cook cutting raw chicken on a board, then using the same board and knife, unwashed, to slice cucumber for a salad that's about to go out.
>
> **What do you do and say?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Stops the salad going out straight away, and has it thrown out and made again
2. Cleans and sanitises the board, knife and bench, or has them replaced
3. Explains calmly why: raw meat can carry bacteria onto food that won't be cooked
4. Shows the right way, such as separate boards and knives for raw and ready to eat food or the venue's colour system
5. Treats it as teaching, not a telling off in front of everyone, and lets the senior chef know if it keeps happening

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Lets the salad go out because it's only a small risk
- Says nothing to the junior cook
- Humiliates them in front of the kitchen

## 6. Kitchen: workplace injury

*Id: `kitchen-burn-on-the-line`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Busy service. A colleague burns their forearm on an oven door. It's red and blistering, and they say they're fine and try to keep working.
>
> **What do you do?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Stops them working and gets the burn under cool running water straight away, for about 20 minutes
2. Gets the first aider or manager, and medical help if the burn is large, deep or on a sensitive area
3. Doesn't put ice, butter or creams on it, and covers it loosely afterwards as first aid training says
4. Covers their section so service keeps going without pressure on them to return
5. Makes sure the injury is recorded following the venue's incident procedure

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Lets them carry on working
- Puts ice or butter on the burn

## 7. Management: no shows

*Id: `mgmt-two-no-shows`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Saturday, 5:15pm. Two staff rostered for a 5:30 start haven't arrived or answered their phones. You have 180 covers booked and a function at 7.
>
> **What do you do in the next hour, and afterwards?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Keeps trying to reach them, and calls in other staff in the venue's usual way
2. Reshapes the floor with who is there: moves people into the most important roles and works the floor themselves
3. Makes changes to protect service, such as closing a section, pausing walk ins or talking timing through with the kitchen
4. Briefs the team calmly before service so everyone knows the plan
5. Afterwards, finds out what happened privately and fairly before deciding anything, following the venue's policy and documenting it

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Takes it out on the staff who did turn up
- Decides the missing staff are in trouble before knowing why

## 8. Management: violent incident

*Id: `mgmt-fight-at-the-door`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> 11pm on a busy Friday. Two guests start throwing punches near the entrance. Other guests are filming, and a staff member is moving towards them.
>
> **What do you do, during and after?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Puts people's safety first: keeps staff from stepping in physically unless they're trained to, and gets security
2. Calls the police on 000 if anyone is hurt, weapons are involved or it can't be calmed quickly
3. Separates the groups and moves other guests away, and stops serving everyone involved
4. Gets first aid to anyone hurt
5. Keeps the CCTV, writes up the incident and records it as the venue's licence requires, with witness details
6. Checks on the staff afterwards and talks through what happened

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Wades in alone, or sends a junior staff member in
- Lets those involved keep drinking, or doesn't record it

## 9. Management: cash variance

*Id: `mgmt-till-short`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Closing the bar, the till is $180 short. Three staff used that till tonight.
>
> **How do you handle it, tonight and over the next few days?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Recounts and looks for honest causes first: the float, voids, refunds, card payments put through as cash, a miscounted drop
2. Follows the venue's cash handling procedure, and records the shortfall and what was checked
3. Doesn't accuse anyone, and talks to each person who used the till privately and calmly
4. Uses the venue's records, such as the point of sale report and CCTV where the policy allows, before drawing conclusions
5. Fixes the process if it allowed the mistake, such as one person per till or counts at changeover, and tells the owner if it happens again

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Accuses a staff member in front of others
- Takes the money out of staff pay

## 10. Management: standards conflict

*Id: `mgmt-free-pouring`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> Your most experienced bartender keeps free pouring spirits, although the venue requires measured pours. When reminded, they laugh it off and say they're more accurate by eye than any jigger.
>
> **How do you deal with it?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Talks to them privately, not in front of the team or guests
2. Explains why the standard exists: responsible service of alcohol, cost, and consistency for guests
3. Listens to their view, and sets the expectation clearly and plainly

- Tells them off in front of the team

## 11. Management: escalated complaint

*Id: `mgmt-bill-complaint`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> A server asks you to come to a table. The guest is furious about a $40 mistake on the bill, says the server was rude about it, and threatens to leave a bad online review.
>
> **How do you handle the guest, and then the server?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Listens without interrupting, stays calm, and apologises for the experience
2. Checks the bill and fixes any mistake on the spot
3. Offers something to make it right that fits the problem and the venue's policy, without being pushed into more by the threat
4. Thanks the guest and makes sure they leave feeling heard
5. Talks to the server privately afterwards to hear their side and work out how it happened, without blame

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Argues with the guest or takes the server's side in front of them
- Gives away a large amount just to stop the review
- Tells the server off in front of the guest

## 12. Management: new starter

*Id: `mgmt-first-shift`. Not live yet.*

**The applicant sees:**

> A new floor staff member starts this Friday night, your busiest service. They have some hospitality experience, but not at your venue.
>
> **How do you set them up to succeed on their first shift?**

**A strong answer covers** (the AI marker ticks these off; any reasonable approach that meets the point counts):

1. Checks before the shift that they hold what the role needs in your state, such as an RSA certificate, and that their paperwork is done
2. Gives them an induction before service: the layout, emergency exits and procedures, allergens, and how orders work
3. Pairs them with an experienced staff member and gives them a sensible section or role for a first night
4. Checks in during the shift and makes it easy for them to ask for help
5. Gives them feedback after the shift, and plans what they'll learn next

**Red flags** (cap the safety or judgement mark at 2 out of 5):

- Puts them on a full section alone with no induction
- Doesn't check they hold the certificates the role needs
